02 · Tokenization

One vocabulary, four languages

A single 10,000-token BPE vocabulary shared across India's Wikipedia article in English, Hindi, Telugu, and Maithili. A good tokenizer should not be fluent in one language and clumsy in the others, so the score rewards tokenizing all four equally efficiently: 1000 / (X_max − X_min), where X is tokens per unit of text. Flip between tokenizers, read every token, and paste your own text to watch it being cut up.

A shorthand with a fixed budget

A tokenizer is a shorthand. It gets to invent 10,000 symbols, and all four languages share them. Chunks of text that come up often earn a symbol of their own; everything else has to be spelled out in smaller pieces. If English takes most of the symbols, English is written compactly and Telugu gets spelled out bit by bit.

Fertility is how many pieces it takes to write a given amount of text — lower is better. And the assignment's score is 1000 ÷ (worst fertility − best fertility), which measures only how close the four languages are to each other. It never asks whether the shorthand is any good. That gap between what is measured and what matters is where the next two figures live.

The four articles are nothing like the same size

Before any of it makes sense: this is the corpus. One Wikipedia article per language, and English is thirty-two times the size of Maithili. A weight like ×3 means that language's article is fed to the trainer three times over — which is how a 1.8% article gets any say at all.

Hiding a fifth of the corpus does not settle it either

The usual answer to tuning-against-your-own-test is to hold text back. So: hide a fifth of every article, train on the rest, score only the hidden part. Then change which fifth you hide, and watch the ranking rearrange itself.

Fig. 2 — three recipes, five ways of holding text back
Fifteen training runs — three recipes × five slices. The corpus is four articles; a fifth of that is too little text for a score built on the gap between the two extreme languages to settle down.

So two of the three obvious ways to pick a winner do not work. The score can be bought by making the tokenizer worse, and the held-out score is too noisy here to rank anything. What is left is what does not move: the score on the whole corpus, and the total number of pieces. On both, the submitted recipe is the best of the honest family — and it is not the biggest number on this page.
